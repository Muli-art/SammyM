Full Biography

Making art that
refuses to forget

Born in Nairobi, Kenya, Sammy Mutinda grew up between the red dust of the city's edges and the dense social fabric of its interior. His early years were shaped by the rhythms of communal life, storytelling passed between generations, and a landscape that refused simple categorisation — neither purely rural nor wholly urban.

He studied Fine Art at the University of Nairobi before undertaking advanced study in printmaking and visual culture. His formative encounters with the archive — with what is documented, what is excluded, and what persists only in the body — became the conceptual spine of his practice.

"I am not making images of Africa. I am making images of time — time that has weight, texture, and refusal."

Mutinda's paintings move between abstraction and figuration, often resisting resolution. Surfaces are built slowly — layer upon layer of acrylic, oil, and found pigment — so that looking at a finished work is also an archaeology. Forms emerge and retreat. Figures exist in states of becoming rather than arrival.

His printmaking practice parallels his paintings but with a distinct physical philosophy: the press as translator, the plate as memory. Editions are small, often unique. He is fascinated by the irreproducibility that hides inside every supposedly reproducible medium.

Installation work allows Mutinda to extend his concerns into space. Objects, cloth, earth, and sound are gathered and arranged in ways that transform a gallery room into a site of encounter — not exhibition. Visitors are not positioned as viewers but as participants in an ongoing conversation between matter and meaning.

His drawings — carried always in sketchbooks that have become their own form of archive — are rapid, searching, and densely observational. They record the world with the urgency of someone who knows that ordinary moments are always on the verge of disappearing.

Mutinda has shown extensively across East Africa, Europe, and North America. He has been the subject of solo exhibitions at Nairobi's Circle Art Gallery and residencies in Berlin, Lagos, and New York. His work is held in private and institutional collections across four continents.

He lives and works in Nairobi, Kenya.
