Three bodies of practice — each a different conversation between the artist, the material, and the world. Printmaking. Installation. Drawing.

I — The Press as Memory

Printmaking

Mutinda's printmaking begins with the plate as archive. Each etching, woodcut, or linocut carries the history of its own making — the pressure, the error, the happy accident. Editions are deliberately small; often unique. The press translates what the hand knows.

01 — Prepare The Plate

Copper, zinc, or linoleum — each surface demands a different relationship. The plate receives the drawing. It remembers everything.

02 — Mark The Incision

The mark is made in reverse. What is taken away — gouged, etched, dissolved — becomes what is seen. Subtraction as language.

03 — Ink The Preparation

Ink is mixed by hand. Sometimes ochre and black, sometimes a deep burgundy. The weight and temperature of the day affects the result.

04 — Press The Transfer

Pressure, felt, paper. The press translates. What emerges is always different from what was intended — and always better for it.

II — Space as Conversation

Installation

Installation allows Mutinda to bring time into physical space. Objects, cloth, earth, sound, and found material are gathered into configurations that transform a gallery into a site of encounter — not display. You are not a viewer. You are part of the work.

"The room becomes
the work"

Mutinda constructs installations that take their materials seriously — earth brought from specific places, cloth that has absorbed specific histories, objects that carry specific uses. Nothing is decorative. Everything has been chosen for what it knows.

The work asks the visitor to slow down. To sense the weight of the gathered things. To understand that they are standing inside an argument — one that is patient, persistent, and refuses resolution.

III — The Urgent Line

Drawing

The sketchbook is always open. Drawing is how Mutinda thinks — quickly, urgently, without resolution. Figures appear in the middle of transformation. Landscapes compress years. The line is the record of the hand in real time.

Archive — 2013 to present

47+

sketchbooks filled

The sketchbook is Mutinda's primary tool — a running archive of observation, experiment, and abandonment. Carried everywhere. Nothing is edited before it is entered. The drawings are fast and searching, dense with correction, alive with the energy of not-yet-knowing. From these pages, everything else grows.
